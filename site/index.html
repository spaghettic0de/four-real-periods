01 / THE RESULT

Enough capacity is only part of the problem.

Suppose four tasks share one machine. Each task has a real-valued period a. A valid schedule must execute it at least ℓ times in every window of ⌈ℓa⌉ slots, for every positive integer ℓ. Average rates alone do not guarantee this.

THEOREM · FORMALIZED FOR SORTED INPUT

If 0 < a₀ ≤ a₁ ≤ a₂ ≤ a₃ and
1/a₀ + 1/a₁ + 1/a₂ + 1/a₃ ≤ 5/6,
there exists a valid schedule for all integer times.

This extends the published three-value case. The general real-period conjecture remains open in the sources checked. Sorting and relabeling arbitrary inputs, and the corollary for arbitrary multiplicities of at most four distinct values, are mathematical reductions outside the present Lean statement.

02 / THE PROOF

Turn an infinite question into finite certificates.

Write each task's requested rate as r = 1/a. On the density-5/6 boundary, four rates form a three-dimensional space. Three balanced constructions cover part of it. Another 47 certified cyclic words cover the rest.

For a word of length P, check every starting position and every window shorter than P, together with its average-rate bound. A longer window is complete periods plus one remainder. That is how a finite certificate proves every window, forever.

The point cloud depicts sampled instances at density 5/6; it is an illustration, not the proof. Coverage of the entire region—including strict boundaries—is established by 136 case splits in Lean. Zero-rate faces are drawn only to show the boundary of the space.

03 / CHECK IT

The proof is the artifact.

The final theorem uses only Lean's standard axioms: propositional extensionality, classical choice, and quotient soundness.
